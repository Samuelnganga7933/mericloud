When you would need it

Businesses where staff copy data between email, sheets, CRM, chat and accounting, and where lead follow-up depends on someone remembering.

The problems it solves

  • Leads wait hours because someone has to notice them
  • Data is entered two or three times in different tools
  • When a step fails nobody knows until a customer complains
How it works

An automation is a workflow: something starts it, rules decide the path, and every step reports back.

1

Trigger

A new email, form, payment, row or schedule starts the workflow.

2

Conditions

Rules decide the path: who the lead is, how big the order is, which team owns it.

3

Actions

Create records, send messages, update sheets, call other systems.

4

Approvals

A person confirms the steps that should never be fully automatic.

5

Run log

Each run is recorded with its result so you can see what happened.

6

Alerts

Failures notify a person immediately and retry where it is safe.

How we build it
STEP 1Map the process

We follow one real task from start to finish and note every handover.

STEP 2Build the workflow

Each step is built, tested on real data and shown running.

STEP 3Add safety

Retries, approvals and failure alerts before it goes live.

STEP 4Hand over

You get the run history, documentation and a way to pause or change it.

What you receive
  • Working workflows running on your data
  • Integrations between your existing tools
  • Run history and failure alerts
  • Documentation of every step
  • Training for the people who will own it

Typical technology: n8n, REST APIs, webhooks, Google Workspace, Telegram

Work moves between your tools on its own, and a person is told the moment it cannot.

Which tools can you connect?

Anything with an API, a webhook or an email address, including Google Workspace, CRMs, messaging apps and accounting tools.

What if a step fails?

Can we run it ourselves?

Yes. We can self-host the automation platform so workflows and data stay under your control.
