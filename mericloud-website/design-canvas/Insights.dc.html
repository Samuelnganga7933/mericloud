Technology decisions

Build, buy or connect: how to decide

Technical consultancyAutomationSoftware engineering

Most businesses facing a software decision have three options, and the right one is rarely the one the first quote assumes.

Buy when the need is common. Accounting, email and basic payroll are solved problems, and custom software for them costs more without doing the job better.

Connect when the tools already exist but do not talk to each other. If staff copy data from one screen to another, the missing piece is usually a link, not a new system. An integration or an automation costs a fraction of a platform.

Build when the way you operate is your advantage: a delivery model, a pricing method or a case-handling process that competitors do not share. That deserves software that fits it exactly.

Three questions help. Is this process the same as everyone else's? What does the manual workaround cost each week? What happens if the vendor changes its pricing or its product?

If the answers are unclear, a short technical assessment is cheaper than choosing wrongly.

E-commerce

What an order tracking system actually tracks

E-commerce and business systemsAutomation

An order looks like one event. It is a chain: placed, paid, reserved from stock, picked, packed, handed to a courier, delivered, sometimes returned or refunded.

A tracking system records each link, who made it and when. That record answers the questions that otherwise cost an hour each: where is this order, who packed it, why is the stock count wrong, was it paid?

Four things need to be tracked together. Orders, with a status that only moves forward through a defined path. Stock, reserved at payment and reduced at dispatch. Deliveries, with updates from the courier or rider. Money, matched to the order it belongs to.

Tracked separately, they drift apart. Tracked in one system, they cannot disagree.

The practical test is simple: pick any order from last week and ask how long it takes to find its whole story.

Working with us

What to prepare before you contact a software company

Technical consultancyMaintenance and recovery

You do not need a specification. You need a clear enough picture of the problem for a conversation to be useful.

Write down what is slow, broken or missing, in the words your team would use. Note who does the work today and how long it takes. List the tools involved, including spreadsheets and chat groups.

Gather examples: a typical order, a form, a screenshot of the screen that frustrates people. One real example is worth a page of description.

Decide what a good outcome looks like. Fewer errors, faster replies, one view of the stock: any of these is enough.

Be ready to talk about constraints: a deadline, a budget range, systems that cannot change, people who must approve.

If you cannot answer some of these, say so. Working it out together is part of the first conversation.
